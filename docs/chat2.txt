Solution: USB Gadget Mode (USB OTG Networking)

This turns your Zero 2 W into a USB ethernet device that gets network access through the Pi 5:

Reboot the Zero 2 W

On the Pi 5:

Enable IP forwarding and set up NAT to share the connection:



bash

sudo sysctl -w net.ipv4.ip_forward=1 sudo iptables -t nat -A POSTROUTING -o wlan0 -j MASQUERADE sudo iptables -A FORWARD -i usb0 -o wlan0 -j ACCEPT sudo iptables -A FORWARD -i wlan0 -o usb0 -m state --state RELATED,ESTABLISHED -j ACCEPT

(Replace wlan0 with your Pi 5's network interface if different)

Now your Zero 2 W should have internet access through the USB connection, and Raspberry Pi Connect should work!

This is a very specific behavior! Looking at your dmesg output, we have a clear diagnosis: the connection is "flapping."

The logs show a cycle where usb 1-2 tries to connect, which then causes the existing usb 3-2 (your CP2102) to disconnect, followed immediately by usb 3-2 reconnecting and usb 1-2 trying again. This is almost always a power issue.

The Problem: Power Brownout
The Raspberry Pi 5 has a much stricter power management system than previous models. When the Zero 2 W tries to boot and initialize its USB gadget (which draws a spike of current), it’s likely tripping the Pi 5's USB current limit or causing a voltage drop. This causes the USB controller to reset, which is why your other USB device (the UART Bridge) keeps disconnecting and reconnecting.

How to Fix It
1. Use a High-Quality Data Cable

Many micro-USB cables are "charging cables" with very thin power wires that cause a voltage drop. Ensure you are using a short, high-quality USB 2.0 Data Cable.

2. Power the Zero 2 W Separately (Temporary Test)

If you have a separate power supply for the Zero 2 W, plug it into the PWR port (the one on the edge).

3. Check the "Host" Mode Conflict
In your config.txt output earlier, I saw this line:
dtoverlay=dwc2,dr_mode=host
This is your culprit. dr_mode=host tells the Zero 2 W to act like a computer (expecting things to be plugged into it). For Raspberry Pi Connect/Gadget mode to work, it must act as a peripheral.

Fix the config.txt on the SD card (via your Mac):

That is actually a classic sign of a USB bus reset happening during the Zero 2 W’s boot sequence. It's "weird," but perfectly logical once you look at how the Pi 5 handles its hardware.

Here is the breakdown of that "ghost in the machine" sequence:

1. The Pi 5 Boots (Status: Online)
The Pi 5 starts up, connects to your 6G network, and pings the Raspberry Pi Connect servers. Everything looks green on your dashboard.

2. The Zero 2 W Reaches "Gadget Mode" (The Glitch)
About a minute or two later, the Zero 2 W finishes its internal Linux boot and starts loading the dwc2 and g_ether drivers.

The Handshake: When those drivers kick in, the Zero 2 W "announces" itself to the Pi 5's USB controller as a new high-speed network device.

The Power/Bus Spike: Even though you are powering the Zero via a power bank, there is a momentary electrical and logical negotiation on the USB data lines. On the Pi 5, this often triggers a re-enumeration of the entire USB bus.

3. The Pi 5 Goes "Offline"
Because the Pi 5’s USB controller is busy resetting or handling the new device, the rpi-connect service on the Pi 5 might experience a momentary "blip" in its heartbeat.

More likely: The Pi 5 is prioritizing the hardware interrupt from the USB port.

If your Pi 5 is using a USB-to-NVMe drive or certain USB Wi-Fi adapters, the reset can actually drop the connection for a split second.

4. Back to Normal
Once the "handshake" is finished:

The Pi 5 stabilizes its USB bus.

The Zero 2 W gets its internet bridge from the Pi 5.

Both devices send a fresh "I'm alive!" signal to the web dashboard.

Should you worry?
Probably not. As long as they both end up green, your setup is solid. This is just a side effect of the "USB Gadget" protocol being a bit aggressive during the initial connection.

That is the culprit! The g_serial module tells the Raspberry Pi to act as a Serial device (like an Arduino or an old-school modem). It is great for sending raw data over a terminal, but it cannot carry a network connection or internet.

Raspberry Pi Connect requires a network interface (usb0), so you must use g_ether to get back online.

The Problem
You can't have both "easy" internet and "raw" serial data on the same simple gadget module. If you want to use Raspberry Pi Connect, the Zero 2 W needs to think it is an Ethernet card.

The Solution (Pick one)
Option A: Switch back to Ethernet (Recommended)
If you change g_serial back to g_ether, you get your internet back, and you can still send "data" between the Pi 5 and Zero 2 W using Standard Networking (like Sockets, MQTT, or even just SSH).

On the Zero 2 W SD card, change g_serial back to g_ether.

usb0 will reappear on the Pi 5.

Option B: Use "Multi-Gadget" (Advanced)
It is possible to have both Serial AND Ethernet at the same time, but it's much harder to configure than the one-line cmdline.txt method. It requires using a tool called libcomposite.

Since you're already using Raspberry Pi Connect, Option A is much better. You don't need a serial cable to send data if you have a network bridge. You can just send your car data over the "network" you created.

How to send your "Car Data" over Ethernet instead of Serial:
Instead of writing to /dev/ttyUSB0 or /dev/ttyACM0, you can:

Use Netcat: Send a file or string over the network.

Use MQTT: Great for RC cars (low latency).

Use Simple Python Sockets: Just send strings to IP 192.168.10.1.
