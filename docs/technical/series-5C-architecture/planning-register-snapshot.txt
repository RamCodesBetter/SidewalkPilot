# SidewalkPilot Series 5: 20 Major Decisions

## November 21, 2026 Architecture and Precision Clarification

The student reconfirmed ConvNeXt-Tiny with a modest U-Net-style decoder and
explicit skip connections as the primary model. The next beta, 5.C, uses FP32
for model parameters, forward computation, gradients, optimizer tensors, and
PyTorch evaluation. AMP, FP16, BF16, and TF32 are disabled for this model.
This supersedes Decision 9's mixed-precision training choice and defers Decision
10's FP16/INT8 deployment optimization ladder unless the student requests it
again. FP32 ONNX export and Jetson runtime checks are still required; an engine
or field-ready model is not claimed by this clarification.

Existing 5.A and 5.B artifacts remain LR-ASPP/MobileNetV3-Large binary baselines,
trained with BF16 autocast and saved in FP32. They are not the locked primary
ConvNeXt/U-Net architecture and are not retroactively renamed or converted.
The MobileNetV3 architecture remains a lightweight comparator candidate. A fair
comparison still requires matching data, resolution, loss, and evaluation.

The 5.C implementation currently predicts sidewalk/background because the current
labeling work is binary. It does not establish that the planned twelve-class
taxonomy or complete September training/control recipe is implemented. The
architecture has 28,855,298 parameters and passed a local FP32 setup smoke check;
full training, deployment, and accuracy/latency qualification remain pending.

**Decision: A.** Use an 80% training, 10% validation, and 10% test similarity-group split.

Keeping a group together means that near-duplicate frames do not cross training,

## 6. Automatic Labeling and Human Quality Control [LOCKED: MODIFIED A]

**Option A (revised September 12):** Generate segmentation labels for the current Series
3/4 images with Grounded SAM 2, review an initial 200 masks, and review 200 additional
masks if the first sample does not meet the selected threshold. Preserve indexed PNG
masks, review records, and label provenance.

**Option B:** Accept all automatically generated masks as ground truth with only visual

**Option C:** Draw and correct every mask manually.

**Decision: modified A with 200 plus 200 review, September 12.** Generate segmentation
labels for each image in the current Series 3/4 dataset using Grounded SAM 2. Review
200 generated masks and correct identified errors. If at least 80% (160 of 200) were
correctly labeled, do not trigger a dataset-wide relabel. Otherwise review 200 additional

Compute the review fractions before applying corrections and retain the corrected masks

These are student-selected sample-review thresholds, not measured segmentation accuracy
or proof that every unreviewed mask is correct. The 75% second-sample threshold is less
strict than the initial 80% threshold. Sample selection, the definition of a correctly
labeled mask, and the review after a relabel remain to be specified before execution.
Automatically generated masks are reference labels; calling them ground truth requires
documenting their verification. IoU, precision, recall, and F1 evaluate segmentation
predictions against those references, not raw IMU or encoder readings.

**Option B:** Use only a lightweight MobileNet-style encoder to minimize latency.

**Option C:** build a large custom encoder-decoder from scratch.

**Decision: A.** Use an ImageNet-pretrained ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder with a modest U-Net-style

Upsample logits to the model-input resolution. Train one MobileNetV3-based comparator

**Option B:** Copy Series 3 hyperparameters, batch size, augmentation, and loss directly.

**Decision: A.** Use AdamW with pretrained-encoder warmup, lower-rate encoder unfreezing,
cosine decay with warmup, mixed-precision training with finite-loss and finite-gradient

and restrained image/mask-consistent augmentation. Establish weighted focal loss as the
baseline and retain focal plus Dice only if frozen validation evidence improves. Select
checkpoints with a safety-aware score covering critical-class recall, macro IoU, boundary
quality, false-drivable error, and planner validity rather than pixel accuracy alone.

## 11. Mask Decoding, Confidence, and Temporal Stabilization [LOCKED: A]

**Option A:** Convert logits to softmax probabilities, use argmax for the semantic mask,
and measure uncertainty in the ground region and proposed driving corridor. Establish a
single-frame baseline, then add only short confidence-aware stabilization that does not
delay new obstacles.

**Option B:** Use argmax IDs only and discard all probability information.

**Option C:** heavily average masks over time for maximum visual smoothness.

**Decision: A.** Compute per-pixel softmax probabilities and select classes with
`argmax(softmax(logits))`. Retain probabilities to measure maximum confidence, top-two
margin, and entropy in the ground region, sidewalk boundary, obstacle boundary, and
proposed driving corridor. Establish a single-frame baseline. Add only short,
confidence-aware stabilization if measured flicker requires it, and allow newly detected
obstacles to affect the latest result immediately rather than waiting for an average.

## 12. Semantic Mask to Metric Free Space [LOCKED: A]

**Option A:** Apply the versioned class-policy table, select free space connected to the
vehicle footprint, project valid ground-contact regions into a metric bird's-eye-view
grid, inflate non-drivable space by half vehicle width plus a measured margin, and mark
geometry invalid when safe ground contact cannot be estimated.

**Option B:** Treat every sidewalk-class pixel as directly drivable and plan through the
raw image mask.

**Option C:** Train another network to output the final free-space path directly.

**Decision: A.** Apply the versioned semantic-class policy to produce traversability,
base cost, inflation, and speed effects. Select the free-space component connected to
the vehicle's projected starting region so the planner cannot jump to a disconnected
sidewalk or background region. Project only geometrically valid ground-plane pixels into
a metric bird's-eye-view grid. For above-ground objects, estimate and project the
ground-contact region, inflate it into occupied space, and mark the relevant geometry
unknown when safe ground contact cannot be estimated. Inflate all non-drivable space by
half the measured vehicle width plus a measured safety margin. Preserve free, occupied,
and unknown state together with semantic cost and confidence for the planner.

## 13. Path-Planning Algorithm [LOCKED: A]

**Option A:** Begin with Euclidean clearance in metric bird's-eye view and choose a path
that balances maximum clearance with continuity. Return invalid when minimum clearance,
connected length, confidence, or reachability fails. Add dynamic programming only if
frozen planner cases expose a weakness.

**Option B:** Choose the maximum-clearance point independently in every image row.

**Option C:** Implement A*, dynamic programming, or another global planner before creating
the simpler baseline.

**Decision: A.** Compute Euclidean obstacle clearance in the metric bird's-eye-view grid
and select a forward path that rewards clearance and progress while penalizing lateral
jumps, curvature, semantic risk, and uncertain space. Constrain candidate points to
reachable connected free space. Return no valid path when minimum clearance, reachable
length, confidence, or geometric validity fails. Establish and test this continuity-aware
baseline first; introduce dynamic programming or another global search method only when
frozen planner cases demonstrate a specific failure that the added method resolves.

## 14. Steering Controller [LOCKED: A]

**Option A:** Have the Jetson return compact metric waypoints and run Pure Pursuit on the
Raspberry Pi 5. Begin with a fixed look-ahead at low fixed speed, then make look-ahead
speed-adaptive. Output logical steering, apply the existing physical calibration once,
and enforce steering and steering-rate limits before considering additional EMA.

**Option B:** Convert image-center error directly into proportional steering.

**Option C:** Begin with Stanley control or model-predictive control.

**Decision: A.** The Jetson Orin Nano returns compact metric path waypoints and the
Raspberry Pi 5 runs Pure Pursuit. Begin at low fixed speed with a fixed look-ahead,
calculate path curvature from a selected metric waypoint, and convert curvature into a
logical steering angle using the measured wheelbase. Apply the existing physical servo
calibration exactly once, followed by steering-angle and steering-rate limits. Introduce
speed-adaptive look-ahead only after the fixed configuration passes controlled tests;
add further steering smoothing only when measured oscillation requires it.

## 15. Normal Throttle and Speed Control [LOCKED: MODIFIED A]

**Option A:** Begin field control at one low fixed speed, then compute deterministic target
speed or throttle limits from path curvature, clearance, confidence, visibility, path
validity, and Hall-sensor speed. Series 5 may accelerate, slow, or stop.

**Option B:** Add a learned throttle head immediately.

**Option C:** Keep one fixed throttle permanently.

**Decision: modified A using normalized throttle rather than MPH.** Series 5 owns normal
forward-throttle control using a normalized command from `0.0` to `1.0`, where `0.0`
requests no propulsion and `1.0` means 100% of the configured safe maximum. Begin field
testing at one low fixed command. After steering is stable, deterministically reduce or
zero the command for high curvature, low clearance, low confidence, limited visible/reachable
path, or an invalid path, and permit a bounded increase only on a clear, sufficiently
straight, confident path. Hall-sensor measurements may verify and stabilize behavior,
but the initial policy does not require an MPH target. Do not add a learned throttle head
until a deliberately collected variable-speed dataset can support one. Zero throttle
alone does not establish that the vehicle has stopped; existing safety-stop behavior
remains separate from normal steering and throttle control.

## 17. LiDAR AEB, Manual Takeover, and Failure Behavior [LOCKED: A]

**Option A:** Series 5 owns normal steering and speed; LiDAR owns only independent AEB.
The existing AEB toggle can fully disengage LiDAR intervention during controlled tests
while measurements remain logged. Manual takeover always overrides autonomy. Stale,
mismatched, invalid, or no-path results cause a controlled stop rather than reuse without
limit.

**Option B:** Remove LiDAR before Series 5 field testing.

**Option C:** Fuse LiDAR into Series 5 steering and free-space planning.

**Recommendation: A.** It tests camera-based normal avoidance while retaining a
controllable independent emergency layer.

**Decision: A.** Series 5 owns normal steering and normalized forward-throttle control.
LiDAR does not steer or shape the normal Series 5 path; it remains an independent AEB
layer that can slow or stop the car when enabled. The existing AEB toggle fully disables
LiDAR intervention during deliberate, supervised model-only tests while LiDAR measurements
and potential-intervention events continue to be recorded. Manual steering, throttle,
braking, and quit commands always override autonomy. A stale response, model or protocol
mismatch, invalid geometry, invalid path, insufficient path confidence, or missing Jetson
response causes a controlled stop instead of reusing a path without limit. Re-enabling AEB
restores LiDAR intervention immediately without changing the Series 5 model or planner.
